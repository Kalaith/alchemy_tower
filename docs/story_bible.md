# Story Bible — Alchemy Tower

**Status: locked.** This document does not invent the story. The arcs, reaction
lines, journal beats and epilogue were written first, and between them they
already commit to a specific history with specific numbers in it. What follows
is that history written down in one place, so the next twenty hours of content
stay consistent with the first twenty.

Where the shipped content genuinely leaves something open, it is marked
**OPEN** and left open. Two of those are open on purpose and should stay that
way.

Everything asserted here is traceable to authored text. The bracketed ids are
the beats that say it, so a claim can be checked rather than trusted.

---

## 1. The wizard

**The wizard did not fail, flee, or vanish.** They sealed the tower floor by
floor, deliberately, and left. `[archive_revelation]`, `[observatory_ending]`

**Why.** The tower is a machine of rooms that binds living systems into itself.
Run at full, it consumes them. The sealing was not hoarding and not
disaster-response — it was the only way to stop the draw.
`[archive_revelation]`, `[observatory_epilogue]`

**How they knew.** For eleven months before the sealing the wizard kept working
notes: yields falling, wards drawing more than they returned. The hand is
unhurried. It is a proof, not a confession — somebody quietly establishing to
their own satisfaction what the tower would do if left running.
`[erased_notes_raised]`, `[eleven_months_restored]`

**The margin note.** The same three words appear in the margin of four separate
pages: *"not yet, then"*. `[eleven_months_restored]`

**The removal.** The wizard took those eleven months out of the record
themselves, and did it *after* writing them. Ione's reading — which the game
endorses by making it the reconciled record — is that the pages were removed not
to hide the decision but to stop anyone finding the *working* that led to it and
being persuaded by it. The wizard did not want their reasoning inherited.
`[the_previous_hand]`

**The thesis of the ending.** Restraint did not mean leaving the floors shut. A
tower used carefully is a different thing from a tower run. The player's whole
campaign is the counter-argument to the wizard's caution, and the ending grants
it: *"The tower is not yours to conquer. It is yours to tend."*
`[observatory_epilogue]`, epilogue beat 30

**OPEN — deliberately.** Whether the wizard is alive, and where they went. No
authored line commits either way. Leave it.

## 2. The failed intervention

The failure the story turns on is not an explosion. It is a slow draw that
nobody in the valley attributed correctly, because its effects arrive as
ordinary bad luck:

- Yields falling. `[eleven_months_restored]`
- Wards drawing more than they returned. `[eleven_months_restored]`
- Ground failing *underneath* the valley, not on it — Rowan's flushed trays come
  up as empty as the sown ones, so the seed is sound. `[harvest_blight_traced]`
- Pollinators at eleven where the previous keeper's log recorded ninety. Not a
  habitat problem; a valley problem. `[pollinator_count_taken]`
- Two years of headaches along the well row, written down by everybody
  including the apothecary as strain. It was the water. `[water_source_identified]`

**The shape of every diagnosis in the game is the same:** somebody has been
blaming themselves or the weather for a systemic effect, and the tower is what
makes the measurement possible. Mira measures water against a mirrorsalt pour.
Lyra counts two marked rows for four nights. Rowan flushes trays against sown
ones. Brin digs under two feet of mortar. Keep this shape. New content should
diagnose before it fixes.

## 3. The ecosystem model

One rule, and it explains the whole valley:

> The tower's wards bind living systems into the tower. A running tower draws
> from them and returns less than it takes. The draw is valley-wide and slow,
> and it looks like ordinary decline.

Consequences that are already load-bearing and must stay true:

- **Sealing stopped the draw.** Twenty years of the valley not getting worse,
  and not getting better either.
- **Reopening a floor does not restart the draw** — the player uses rooms rather
  than running the machine. This is the distinction the ending rests on.
- **Restoration is to the ground, not to the tower.** The strongest epilogue
  beat says so outright: the flowering valley and the carrying bed rows "were
  not restored to the tower. They were restored to the ground." Anything that
  reads as the tower reclaiming the valley is off-model. Epilogue beat 100
- **Recovery is measurable, and the measurements are the drama.** Eleven versus
  ninety. Two years of headaches. Four generations of the same ground.

## 5. Act structure and reveal order

The acts are already enforced by `minimum_total_brews` on the arc requests and
by the floor gates. This is a description of what is there, not a proposal.

### Act One — the tower helps one person at a time (0–~14 brews)

Nobody is investigating anything. Requests are individual and immediate: a
draught for a headache, a light for a dark walk home, a tonic for the repair
crews. The revelation of the act is small and social — the tower stops being a
hazard and becomes an anecdote, then a line in a ledger.

Beats: `first_true_brew`, `first_town_relief`, `greenhouse_expanded`,
`crews_kept_working`, `tower_entered_the_ledger`.

### Act Two — the problem turns out to be systemic (~14–30 brews)

Every arc's middle beat is a *measurement*, and each one converts a private
failure into a valley-wide fact. This is the act where the game stops being
errands. The floors that reopen here (containment, rune workshop) are what make
the measuring possible.

Beats: `harvest_blight_traced`, `pollinator_count_taken`,
`water_source_identified`, `terraces_still_alive`, `containment_started`,
`containment_stable`, `first_rune_imbuing`.

### Act Three — the record, and restoration to the ground (~30+ brews)

The archive gives up the eleven months, and the wizard's reasoning is
reconstructed — not to be obeyed, but to be answered. In parallel the valley is
restored in the terms Act Two established: pollinators counted back, the well
row quiet, the beds turned, the pass held through a winter.

Beats: `erased_notes_raised`, `record_reconciled`, `eleven_months_restored`,
`the_previous_hand`, `pollinators_returned`, `town_water_cleared`,
`harvest_beds_turned`, `terraces_worked_again`, `pass_kept_open`,
`town_charter_signed`, `worst_case_shelf_cleared`, then
`archive_revelation` → `observatory_ending`.

**Reveal order is strict on one point:** the player must have *measured* the
decline (Act Two) before the archive explains its cause (Act Three). The
eleven-months reveal lands as an answer to a question the player asked, not as
exposition. Content that explains the draw earlier breaks the game's spine.

## 6. The nine townsfolk

Each arc is the same three-part move — a private trouble, a measurement that
makes it public, a fix that changes the valley — and each ends with somebody
allowed to stop carrying something.

| Who | Private trouble | What they measure | What they put down |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mira**, apothecary | A counter she cannot keep up with | The square's water against a mirrorsalt pour | Two years of headaches she had recorded as strain |
| **Rowan**, herbalist | A dark walk home; a failing harvest | Flushed trays against sown ones | Blame for seed that was always sound |
| **Mayor Elric** | A town that closes at sundown | Whether the tower is a hazard or a supplier | Treating the tower as an anecdote |
| **Ione**, archivist | Nine years of notes with nothing outside them | A cordial against a page scraped blank | Working alone in a room somebody went through |
| **Brin**, groundskeeper | Beds that will not take | Two feet of mortar over the tower's terraces | A decade of blaming himself for the ground |
| **Lyra**, keeper | Creatures behaving wrongly | Eleven visitors where the log said ninety | The assumption that it was her habitats |
| **Tarn**, waystation keeper | Eleven years of carts going past | Whether a lit lamp stops anyone | Being the valley the road avoids |
| **Wren**, physician | A shelf for the cases she cannot fix | Whether stock can be relied on | The shelf itself |
| **The Crow** | — | — | Guide only; no arc, no rapport |

**Voice rules that the existing lines already keep.** Nobody makes a speech.
Wren "logged it in one line and underlined the date, which for her is an
outburst." Brin writes the cost under every notice. The valley's register is
understatement, and the emotional payload sits in what people stop doing rather
than what they say. Keep it.

## 7. The unsigned orders

Three unsigned board orders arrive, are filled, and are collected. No fourth
comes. Somebody outside the valley wanted to know whether the tower was working
again, and found out. `[unsigned_order_unanswered]`, epilogue beat 98

**OPEN — deliberately.** Who they are and what they intend. The epilogue says
outright that the question "stays open, and it was never yours to close." Do
not answer it. If a sequel or a later act wants it, that is a decision to take
knowingly; nothing currently shipped commits to anything.

## 8. Writing rules for new content

1. **Diagnose before fixing.** A new arc beat that solves something without
   first measuring it is off-shape.
2. **Restore to the ground, never to the tower.** The tower is the instrument.
3. **No speeches.** Understatement, and let the change be visible in behaviour.
4. **Numbers are load-bearing.** Eleven and ninety, two years, nine years,
   twenty years, eleven months, four generations. Reuse them; do not contradict
   them.
5. **The wizard is answered, not obeyed and not vilified.** They were careful
   and they were wrong about one thing: that the tower could not be used
   carefully by somebody else.
6. **Restraint is a practice, not a prohibition.** Every reopened floor is the
   argument.
